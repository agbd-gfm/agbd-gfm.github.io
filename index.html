Of 13 Geospatial Foundation Models, only the two trained on rich multi-modal, multi-temporal data (AlphaEarth Foundations and TESSERA) beat a fully supervised state-of-the-art model, and the AEF-based model performs on par with the ESA CCI biomass product on independent reference data.

Abstract

Accurate estimation of above-ground biomass (AGB) from satellite imagery is essential for the large-scale monitoring of carbon stocks, yet it remains a challenging regression task at global scale. Geospatial Foundation Models (GFMs) have recently emerged as a promising machine learning paradigm to derive general-purpose representations from Earth observation (EO) data, but their utility for quantitative regression tasks like biomass estimation remains largely unexplored, as most benchmarks focus on classification and segmentation. Here, we benchmark 13 GFMs for above-ground biomass estimation, specifically GEDI L4A biomass values. We compare them against a fully supervised state-of-the-art (SOTA) baseline trained on A Global-scale Biomass Dataset (AGBD), a machine learning-ready benchmark spanning diverse biomes and geographies. Most of the GFMs evaluated substantially underperform with respect to the supervised baseline, whether frozen or fine-tuned; only TESSERA and AlphaEarth Foundations (AEF) features with certain prediction heads yield a better performance. Overall, our SOTA model trained on AEF embeddings and selected raw EO features achieves the best overall result (an RMSE of 50.79 Mg/ha vs. 53.73 Mg/ha on AGBD features). This feature combination also generalizes better than AGBD features: it reduces RMSE by up to 25% in selected regions excluded from training, and, trained on single-year data alone, it outperforms the model trained on multi-year AGBD features. With labels from only 11 regions around the world, the AEF-based model also performs on par with the globally calibrated ESA CCI biomass product on independent reference plots (RMSE 44.79 Mg/ha vs. 45.77 Mg/ha). These findings indicate that GFM features can be highly informative for biomass estimation, when the underlying model is trained on rich multi-modal data. Distributed as accessible embedding layers, they lower the computational barrier to state-of-the-art biomass estimation for practitioners.

Key findings

🛰️

Rich inputs make the difference

Only AEF and TESSERA, which summarize a full year of multi-modal observations per pixel, beat the supervised baseline on AGBD Lite (53.70 and 56.43 vs. 59.02 Mg/ha). The other 11 GFMs fall short, frozen or fine-tuned with LoRA: the best, CROMA (+SAR), reaches 61.56 Mg/ha.

🏆

Best result on AGBD Full

fcn_film on AEF+ reaches 50.79 Mg/ha vs. 53.73 on AGBD features. Even an MLP on AEF (52.22) beats the supervised baseline and the fine-tuned TerraMind (+SAR) model (57.21).

🌍

Better generalization

AEF-based models reduce RMSE by up to 25% in regions held out from training, and, trained on 2019 data alone, they beat AGBD features trained on both years.

📏

On par with ESA CCI

With labels from only 11 regions, the AEF-based model matches the globally calibrated ESA CCI product on independent AGBref plots (RMSE 44.79 vs. 45.77 Mg/ha).

Benchmark of 13 GFMs

We first compare all 13 GFMs on AGBD Lite (≈600k samples), each with a UPerNet head, against the supervised fcn_film baseline. AEF and TESSERA are the only GFMs that beat the baseline trained on all AGBD features (53.70 and 56.43 vs. 59.02 Mg/ha). With all encoders frozen, they have 17–39% and 11–35% lower RMSE than the other 11 GFMs; of those, only SSL4EO-MoCo (64.34 Mg/ha) improves on the Sentinel-2-only baseline (66.51 Mg/ha).

Fine-tuning with LoRA improves every encoder, most of all those whose frozen features performed worst, and 6 of the 11 then beat the Sentinel-2-only baseline. Adding L-band SAR (CROMA, TerraMind, DOFA) or multi-temporal Sentinel-2 (SatlasNet, Prithvi, Prithvi v2) helps further, but less than fine-tuning itself. The best fine-tuned configuration, CROMA (+SAR) at 61.56 Mg/ha, still trails AEF, TESSERA and the multi-modal supervised baseline.

Results on AGBD Full

On AGBD Full (≈16M samples), AEF keeps its lead. An MLP on AEF already beats the supervised baseline, fcn_film on AEF lowers the error to 50.92 Mg/ha, and adding a few ancillary features (AEF+) gives the best result overall. TerraMind (+SAR), chosen because it nearly matches CROMA (+SAR) at about a tenth of the compute, still trails AEF when trained on the full dataset.

Geographical & temporal generalization

AEF embeddings do not just improve average accuracy; they also change how models behave when training and test data differ. When the target region is excluded from training (cross-region), AEF and AEF+ reduce RMSE by 14–25% in Africa and South America. This transfer is not unconditional: in South Asia, AEF alone is 25% worse than raw AGBD features, while AEF+ stays on par with them.

Prediction maps

We generate wall-to-wall AGB maps on zoomed-in windows of six Sentinel-2 tiles spanning distinct biomes and hemispheres, with fcn_film on AEF embeddings and on AGBD features, and compare them with the ESA CCI v6.0 product. Scored against the GEDI L4A footprints inside each window, the AEF-based model has the lowest error on all six tiles. Its maps follow terrain and vegetation structure while staying smooth over homogeneous cover; the AGBD-features baseline shows the same large-scale patterns with more small-scale noise, and the coarser ESA CCI (100 m) diverges most from GEDI.

Comparison with ESA CCI on independent data

To test performance beyond the AGBD test set, we evaluate our fcn_film model on AEF and the operational ESA CCI biomass map on AGBref, an independent global reference dataset built from national forest inventories, research plots and airborne LiDAR, at 10 km resolution. Although our model is trained on only the 11 AGBD regions, it performs on par with the globally calibrated ESA CCI product in R², r and RMSE. ESA CCI has a slightly lower MAE, and the two trade places on bias, which remains small for both.

Takeaways for practitioners

The decisive property of a GFM for biomass estimation is the information its representation encodes. AEF and TESSERA distill a full year of multi-modal observations per pixel; most other GFMs see a single, mostly optical acquisition, and fine-tuning cannot supply the information that is missing. When the representation is rich enough, a lightweight head suffices: an MLP on AEF embeddings beats the supervised state of the art at a fraction of the cost, since the embeddings are pre-computed and no GPU-intensive feature extraction is needed.

Pre-computed embeddings come with constraints. Their producer fixes the input sensors, the temporal aggregation and the spatial resolution, and since AEF's weights are not released, its embeddings cannot be fine-tuned or generated for other time periods. TESSERA, the second-best GFM in our benchmark, releases both its weights and its embeddings, offering an open alternative.
